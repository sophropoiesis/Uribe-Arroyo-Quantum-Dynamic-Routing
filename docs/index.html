01 / Research overview

Abstract

Uribe Arroyo Quantum Dynamic Routing (UAQDR) is a proposed entropy-acquisition and conditioning architecture that combines measurements from multiple quantum random-number generators (QRNGs). QRNG-derived confidential control material determines the order in which later source measurements are collected.

The proposal incorporates per-source health checks, source provenance assumptions, cryptographic conditioning, and conservative failure policies. It asks whether feedback-controlled routing changes measurable fault resilience or information leakage compared with matched static and independently randomized routing approaches.

Limit of the claim. Dynamic routing does not create entropy, prove unpredictability, or inherently prevent side-channel attacks. No cryptographic security advantage has been demonstrated.

02 / Proposed method

Architecture

The system is organized around independently characterized entropy sources, bounded collection epochs, and the separation of public output from private routing state.

Source diversity
Measurements are gathered from separately identified and independently characterized QRNG sources.
Delayed feedback
Previously accepted quantum-derived internal material selects the next bounded acquisition schedule.
Output separation
Measurements are framed and conditioned without double-counting entropy; public output and private control uses remain distinct.

03 / Experimental design

Evaluation and falsification

The proposed architecture requires comparison against one-source, fixed-order multi-source, and independently randomized multi-source baselines. Comparisons should control for hardware, sampling budgets, conditioning, and environmental conditions.

Proposed measures for comparative study
EntropyConservative entropy bounds and source-health outcomes
RobustnessFault isolation and recovery after source degradation
ObservabilityMeasured side-channel leakage and other externally visible differences
CostLatency, throughput, and power consumption

Negative or inconclusive results are informative. Claims of improvement would require reproducible evidence, not simply the presence of a dynamic schedule.
