Abstract

Research summary

Uribe Arroyo Quantum Dynamic Routing (UAQDR) is a proposed entropy-acquisition and conditioning architecture that combines measurements from multiple quantum random-number generators (QRNGs) and employs quantum-derived control material to select the order or route of subsequent measurements. Unlike a static pipeline, the routing schedule changes between acquisition epochs according to private, cryptographically conditioned measurements collected in previous epochs.

The proposed design incorporates per-source health monitoring, source provenance, bounded scheduling, cryptographic conditioning, separation between output and control uses, and conservative failure handling. The central research question is whether this feedback-controlled routing improves measurable robustness, fault isolation, or resistance to specific observables relative to matched multi-source static architectures.

Dynamic scheduling is not itself a source of new entropy, a proof of unpredictability, or a proven side-channel countermeasure. The paper defines a conceptual architecture and falsifiable evaluation plan, not a validated cryptographic primitive.

Keywords Quantum random-number generation; QRNG; entropy pooling; dynamic routing; min-entropy; entropy health tests.

Research record

Research status

  • Architecture and validation questions are described in the v0.1 research draft.
  • Prototype hardware and raw measurement data have not yet been published.
  • Formal security proofs and empirical advantages have not been established.
  • The novelty assessment and bibliography remain preliminary.

Methodology

Proposed evaluation

A meaningful assessment would compare UAQDR against one-source, fixed-order multi-source, and independently randomized multi-source baselines while keeping sampling budgets, source hardware, conditioning, and experimental conditions equivalent.

Measurements of interest include conservative entropy estimates, fault recovery, side-channel leakage, latency, throughput, and energy cost. The purpose is to test whether the scheduling mechanism creates a measurable difference, including the possibility of no improvement.
